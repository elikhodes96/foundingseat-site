01 · The problem

Your first sales hire is a guess until someone tests the market.

3 months

Spent finding who buys

A new salesperson usually spends their first quarter working out which companies and job titles actually say yes.

Bad data

Talking to the wrong people

Scraped lists are full of wrong titles, people who left months ago and emails that bounce. We've felt it ourselves, and most sales leaders we speak to feel it too.

1 year

Of salary on a wrong hire

Without evidence, the job description is a guess. A wrong first hire can cost a year's salary and a year of momentum.

04 · Qualified meetings

You only pay for a meeting when all six are true.

  1. It happened. The prospect turned up. A no-show is rebooked or not charged.
  2. Right company. Inside the target profile we agree at kick-off.
  3. Right person. They decide, or directly influence the decision.
  4. Real problem. They've confirmed they have the problem you solve.
  5. Near-term timing. They expect to decide within 3 months.
  6. They know why they're there. They agreed to a meeting about your product.
Your diary, week 3Illustration

Before each meeting you get a short note: who they are, their problem in their own words and their timing. Dispute any meeting within 24 hours.

05 · What you keep

Six things at the end of week 4, whatever you decide next.

  • Live pipeline

    Every conversation opened, with notes and an agreed next step.

  • Message results

    Two versions tested, which one won and why.

  • Buyer evidence

    Objections heard, answers that worked, what triggers a purchase and how long buyers take.

  • Sales playbook

    Who to sell to, what to say and realistic weekly targets for a salesperson.

  • Hiring recommendation

    Hire now, wait, or don't. If hire: the scorecard, salary range, advert and a mock-call test.

10 · Questions founders ask

Before you get in touch

We already use a recruiter.

Keep them if you like. Four weeks of evidence gives any recruiter a far better brief, and your hire starts with live pipeline.

We need someone now.

The four weeks can run alongside your search. Whoever you hire inherits the pipeline.

Isn't it too early for sales?

That's what the four weeks tests, for £3,500 instead of a salary.

You don't know our sector.

Most candidates won't either. The four weeks is where we learn it, and where we find out what your hire needs to know.

What if it doesn't work?

Stop the four weeks at any time with 7 days' notice and keep everything produced. If outbound doesn't work for you, you've found out before committing a salary.

Who builds the list?

We do, and we check every contact by hand rather than trusting a scraping tool. Your first 100 prospects are part of the four weeks. On Run we build 100 new prospects every month, or work from your own list for £500 a month less.

How often do you contact each prospect?

Up to five times over about three weeks: a personal first message, a short follow-up, something useful such as a relevant list or insight, an email or call, and a final note. We stop the moment someone replies or asks us to, and never send more than two messages a week.

Who sends the messages?

Eli and the Founding Seat team, in your company's name, using messages you approve. We don't buy contact lists or automate LinkedIn.
